About me

From project sites to digital operations.

Before virtual assistance, I worked in architecture and construction. A typical project meant understanding a brief, preparing designs, coordinating suppliers and artisans, following up on work, managing changes and making sure important details were not missed.

That experience taught me something I still use now: a good plan is not enough if tasks are not followed up, information is scattered or nobody is clear about what happens next.

When I moved into virtual assistance, I noticed I was naturally drawn to the same kind of work: organising inboxes, mapping client journeys, building workflows, documenting processes and tracking follow-ups.

The tools have changed, but the way I think has not.

I still look at a project and ask: What needs to happen? What comes next? Who owns it? What could be missed? That is the mindset I bring to the businesses I support.

My project mindset

The way I approach digital work comes from how I learned to approach projects.

01

Understand the brief.
I start by getting clear on the goal, the problem and what a useful outcome should look like.

02

Map the moving parts.
I work out the steps, responsibilities, information and follow-ups that need to connect.

03

Build something usable.
I create the workflow, document or system so it is practical for the person who will actually use it.

04

Check for gaps.
I review the process for unclear steps, missing details and anything that could easily be overlooked.
